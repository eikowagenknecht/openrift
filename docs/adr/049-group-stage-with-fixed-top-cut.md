# ADR-049: Group Stage with Fixed Top Cut

## Context and Problem Statement

Store and league events often run a group stage followed by a single-elimination cut: players are split into small round-robin groups, the best of each group qualify, and the qualifiers play a fixed bracket. The format gives every player a guaranteed number of matches, keeps the field small per table, and ends with a bracket spectators can follow.

OpenRift's pairing engine runs Swiss and pod rounds (ADR-022, ADR-041) as `pod_rounds` with derive-on-read standings. It has no notion of a stage, a group, a locked seed or a bracket. How do we add a group-plus-cut format that reuses the round, result and standings machinery, supports the placement rules such events use, and leaves the house rules some organizers add as options?

## Decision Drivers

- Group rounds and cut rounds must reuse result entry, Bo1/Bo3 scorelines, finalization, re-roll, follow tokens and the pairings pages unchanged.
- Store source facts, derive the rest (ADR-022). Only what must stay historically stable after a random or human decision is written.
- Tournament math stays pure and tested in `packages/shared/src/pairing/`.
- Standings must never reorder on re-read. Random decisions happen once, at generation, and are stored as the generated round.
- Rules a typical organizer does not use are options with conventional defaults.
- No external data source. OpenRift neither fetches nor publishes aggregate meta statistics. A meta value can only be a number the organizer enters, kept per tournament and never displayed.

## Considered Options

1. **A new format on the existing round tables**: group and cut rounds are `pod_rounds` of size-2 pods, group membership is a small new table, everything else derives.
2. **A separate group/bracket data model** beside the pod tables with its own result rows and lifecycle.
3. **Emulating the format with Swiss**: three Swiss rounds with fixed tables, then a cut generated by hand.

## Decision Outcome

We implement option 1. `tournaments.format` gains `group_cut` beside the existing behaviour (`rounds`). A `group_cut` tournament always uses `pairing_style = 'swiss'` and `play_mode = '1v1'`, so every match is a size-2 pod and every downstream surface (results, byes, re-roll, manual edits, follow pages) applies. The group stage is rounds 1 to 3, created together at group generation from a stored group membership; a group's pods for a round are filled when that round is started, by the organizer for every group at once or, as an option, by each group on its own. The cut is rounds 4 onward, generated one at a time from the previous cut round's winners. Seeds are written to the existing `tournament_participants.seed` column when the first cut round is generated and never recomputed.

Option 2 duplicates every lifecycle surface for an entity that is structurally a match, the same reason ADR-041 rejected a separate Swiss model. Option 3 cannot express placement-first qualification, cross-group scope, or a fixed bracket, and leaves the organizer running the cut on paper.

### Consequences

- Good, because a group match, a cross-group match and a bracket match are all one pod row, so result entry and the report token pages need no new code paths.
- Good, because the persisted state is one groups table, three participant columns and the rounds themselves. Standings, match scope, qualification and the bracket tree are derived.
- Good, because the three shared modules (group scheduling, group standings, bracket seeding) are pure functions with fixture-based tests for every valid player count.
- Good, because the house rules (Legend tiebreak, rematch avoidance) are options a plain organizer never sees switched on.
- Good, because with self-paced groups a fast group is never held up by a slow one, and the organizer stops being the person who advances the whole room. Organizers who want the room in step switch it off.
- Bad, because the group stage has no bye and no free player count. Counts of 4n+1 and 4n+3 are refused until a participant is added or dropped.
- Bad, because three rounds are open at once during the group stage. The one-open-round invariant that Swiss and pods rely on gets a `group_cut` branch, and `current_round` reads 3 for the whole group stage.
- Bad, because with self-paced groups anyone holding the report token can start a group's next round. That is the same trust the token already grants for result entry.
- Bad, because a result correction to a group round after the cut exists changes the group standings but not the seeds. The UI shows the divergence; it does not block the edit.
- Bad, because a tie that reaches the meta tier blocks the cut until staff type in values. The tier sits below equal points, an unresolved head-to-head, equal game win rate and an equal Legend count, so this is rare.
- Bad, because the standings presenter, the pairings view and the standings tab gain a third branch beside pod and Swiss.
- Neutral, because the local-search pairing engine is unused by this format. Group rounds are a fixed schedule and cut rounds are winner propagation.

### Confirmation

Shared unit tests pin group sizing for 16 to 32 (valid) and 17 to 31 (refused), the 4-player round robin and the paired-3 schedule invariants (three rounds, three matches, two intra plus one cross, no repeat, everyone active each round, stable mapping), every tiebreak tier including the recursive mini-table and its no-progress fallback, placement-first qualification for every count 16 to 32, and bracket seeding with and without rematch avoidance including the case where not all rematches are avoidable. The API integration test runs an 18-player tournament end to end: generate groups, advance one group to round 3 while another is still on round 1 and assert the cut is refused, report every group, generate the cut, report through the final, then correct a group result and assert the seeds are unchanged. A second case generates groups with the Legend tiebreak on and one participant without a Legend and asserts the refusal names the participant. A third drives a tie to the meta tier, asserts the cut is refused while a value is missing, enters the values and asserts the resulting seeds.

## Design

### Tournament settings

New columns on `tournaments`, all frozen once round 1 exists, like `pairing_style` and `match_format`:

| Column                  | Type    | Default  | Meaning                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | ------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `format`                | text    | `rounds` | `rounds` is today's behaviour; `group_cut` is this ADR                                                                                                               |
| `cut_size`              | integer | 8        | 4, 8 or 16 qualifiers                                                                                                                                                |
| `cut_rematch_avoidance` | boolean | false    | keep group-stage opponents apart in the bracket where possible                                                                                                       |
| `legend_tiebreak`       | boolean | false    | two Legend tiers: rarer Legend in the field wins a tie, then lower organizer-entered meta share; every active participant needs a Legend on file at group generation |
| `groups_self_paced`     | boolean | true     | each group starts its own next round when its results are in; off means the organizer starts each round for every group, as in Swiss                                 |

CHECK: `format = 'rounds' OR (pairing_style = 'swiss' AND play_mode = '1v1')`. Regions stay allowed; they affect nothing in this format because no penalty search runs. Match points use `win_points` / `draw_points` as in Swiss.

The create wizard offers "Group stage + top cut" as a format beside "Swiss" and "Pods" and shows the four options under it. No named presets are stored.

### Groups

```sql
CREATE TABLE tournament_groups (
  id              uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  tournament_id   uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES tournaments(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  label           text NOT NULL,                       -- 'A', 'B', ...
  paired_group_id uuid REFERENCES tournament_groups(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  UNIQUE (tournament_id, label)
);
ALTER TABLE tournament_participants
  ADD COLUMN group_id         uuid REFERENCES tournament_groups(id) ON DELETE SET NULL,
  ADD COLUMN group_slot       integer CHECK (group_slot BETWEEN 0 AND 3),
  ADD COLUMN legend_card_id uuid REFERENCES cards(id) ON DELETE SET NULL;
```

Groups have four players. A count of `4n + 2` yields two paired 3-player groups; every player in those groups plays both group mates and one player of the paired group, so everyone in the tournament plays exactly three matches. Group generation is the round-1 action for this format. It validates the active count (`4n` or `4n + 2`, at least 6, at least the cut size) and, with the Legend tiebreak on, that every active participant has a Legend (below). It then shuffles the active participants with the existing `Random`, assigns groups and slots, links the two 3-player groups to each other, creates the three group-stage `pod_rounds` and fills round 1 for every group in the same transaction. The shuffle is the only random act in the group stage. Everything after it is a fixed table:

- 4-player group, slots 0..3: round 1 pairs (0,1)(2,3), round 2 (0,2)(1,3), round 3 (0,3)(1,2).
- Paired 3-player groups A and B, slots 0..2: round r pairs A[r-1] with B[r-1] and the other two in each group with each other.

A match is cross-group when its two players carry different `group_id`. No scope column.

### Group progression

A group's pods for round 2 or 3 are created when that round is started for the group, which is allowed once all of its pods in the previous round are reported. Who starts it depends on `groups_self_paced`. On, the default: each group advances on its own, and starting is an action on the pairings page for staff and on the report-token page for the players at the table. Off: the organizer starts round 2 and round 3 for every group at once from the pairings page, and the button enables when every group has reported, the same rhythm as a Swiss round. The two paired 3-player groups always start together, because each of their rounds contains a cross-group match. In both modes group-stage rounds are not finalized one by one; generating the cut is the sync point and finalizes all three.

Re-rolling round 1 re-shuffles the groups and is allowed only while no group has started round 2. Re-rolling a group's round 2 or 3 is a no-op re-derivation.

Every group-stage start, by the players at the table or by the organizer for all groups, first shows a confirmation naming the group and round: "Please tell a judge before you start the next round." It appears every time, whether or not deck check is enabled, and says nothing about deck checks, so players cannot tell from the page whether their match was picked for one. Cut rounds are generated by staff and show no notice. Nothing is stored.

### Legend on file

`legend_card_id` is the Legend the participant plays, a card reference, so every printing of a Legend is one identity. Staff edit it on the participants tab until groups are generated, the same way `region` is assigned. Group generation fills empty values from the participant's deck-check entry where one exists. The Legend is tournament information: it shows in standings and the participant list to everyone who can see the tournament, report-token viewers included, independent of the deck-publishing consent, which covers the full list and the cover art only. With the Legend tiebreak on, generation is refused while any active participant has no Legend, naming them; the organizer enters the missing ones or turns the option off for the tournament, which drops both Legend tiers so ties go from game win rate straight to the draw. The option is frozen once round 1 exists, so this is its last change point. Without the option the column is informational.

A count of `4n + 1` or `4n + 3` is refused with "Add or drop one player to fill the groups". Who that extra player is (a reserve, a judge playing) is not modelled; they are a participant.

A participant who drops during the group stage forfeits the remaining group matches, the cross-group match included, as walkovers. A walkover is the existing result shape with placements only: the pod is reported with placement 1 for the opponent and 2 for the dropped player and `game_points` null for both, which the schema already allows. The opponent receives `win_points` and a match win, no games exist, so game win rate is untouched, and already played results stay as they are. The drop action reports a pending pod that way at once; a pod that does not exist yet is created already reported when its round starts. A match between two dropped players is a walkover draw. The match card shows a "Walkover" badge in place of the score.

### Group standings

Computed in `packages/shared/src/pairing/group-standings.ts` from the pod rows of rounds 1 to 3, ignoring cross-group pods. Order inside a group:

1. Match points.
2. Two players tied: head-to-head, then intra-group game win rate, then the two Legend tiers if enabled (field count, then meta share), then the deterministic id hash already used as the final key in `sortedStandingRows`. Participant ids are uuidv7 values assigned at registration that nobody can pick, so the hash order is unpredictable beforehand and stable afterwards, which is what a recorded draw would give.
3. Three or more tied: a mini-table over matches among the tied players, ranked by match points then game win rate. A partition that separates some players keeps that order and recurses into each remaining tied subset (a two-player subset uses rule 2). A mini-table that separates nobody falls through to intra-group game win rate, the Legend tiers, id hash.

Game win rate is games won over games played, from `pod_members.game_points`. Walkovers carry no game points and are excluded from it, while they count in match points and match win rate.

The first Legend tier compares how many active participants share the same `legend_card_id`. Fewer is better. Every participant has one when the tier is reachable, and the column does not change after group generation, so the counts need no snapshot.

The second Legend tier compares the Legends' meta share, a number the organizer enters:

```sql
CREATE TABLE tournament_legend_meta_shares (
  tournament_id  uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES tournaments(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  legend_card_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES cards(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  share          numeric(6, 3) NOT NULL CHECK (share >= 0 AND share <= 100),  -- percent
  PRIMARY KEY (tournament_id, legend_card_id)
);
```

Lower share ranks higher. Nothing is fetched: when a tie reaches this tier and a tied Legend has no row, the tied rows carry `decidedBy: "meta_pending"` and keep their provisional order, the standings show a "Needs meta share" marker with an "Enter meta shares" action for staff, and the cut cannot be generated while any such tie exists in group placement or in the cross-group ordering. The action opens a dialog listing exactly the affected Legends with one numeric input each and writes the rows. Values are per tournament, so a later change on whatever site the organizer consulted changes nothing here. Staff can edit a value later; the standings re-derive like every other correction, and the seed lock protects the cut. The number is never displayed outside the dialog; standings show only the "Meta share" badge.

Every row carries `decidedBy`, the tier that placed it below the row directly above, or null when the points differ. The standings and seeds tables show it as a badge ("H2H", "Mini-table", "GW%", "Legend", "Draw") under a header that names the comparison, so two adjacent badges never read as comparing the same pair.

### Qualification and seeds

Qualification is placement-first: all group winners, then all runners-up, and so on, until `cut_size` players are taken. Within one placement tier players are ordered by match win rate over all three of their matches (the cross-group match included), then game win rate, then the two Legend tiers if enabled, then id hash. No opponent-strength tiebreaks in this format. The first `cut_size` players receive seeds 1..N.

Generating the first cut round requires every group-stage pod reported. It finalizes rounds 1 to 3, writes `seed` on the qualifiers, and creates round 4. Seeds are never rewritten. The `seed` column exists since ADR-033 as a manager-entered integer with no consumer in pairing or standings, so this format gives it its meaning: in a `group_cut` tournament the server writes it and the participant patch rejects a manual `seed` once cut rounds exist. Non-qualifiers stay active and are absent from cut rounds without a bye row, so they collect no bye points.

### Bracket

Cut rounds are ordinary `pod_rounds`. Round 4 holds `cut_size / 2` pods, and pod number is the bracket slot in conventional order (for 8: 1v8, 4v5, 2v7, 3v6). Each later cut round is generated from the previous cut round once it is finalized: pod n of the next round pairs the winners of pods 2n-1 and 2n. The final's winner completes the tournament. The bracket view derives the tree from pod numbers and shows the original seed on every player in every round.

With `cut_rematch_avoidance` off, the first cut round is the conventional bracket. With it on, `packages/shared/src/pairing/cut-bracket.ts` scores each assignment of seeds to slots with four penalties in strict priority: a first-round match between group-stage opponents, a possible second-round meeting between players of the same group, how far each first-round pairing's seed sum strays from the conventional N+1 (so the top seeds keep weak opponents), and the number of seeds not in their conventional slot. For a Top 8 the search is exhaustive over the 8! assignments; for a Top 16 it is the engine's swap descent from the conventional bracket. Seeds are identities and never change; only slots move. The result is deterministic for given seeds and groups.

The higher original seed chooses who starts in every cut match. The bracket and the match cards show the seed on each side and name the chooser. It is a display rule, not a stored one.

### Lifecycle and edits

`status` is unchanged. `current_round` counts created rounds, so it reads 3 through the whole group stage and each group's own progress derives from which of its pods exist. The stage of a round is derived: rounds 1 to 3 are the group stage, later rounds the cut. Group standings, per-group progress, qualification and the bracket are read from one standings endpoint that branches on `format`.

Results of group rounds stay editable after the cut is generated, as every result in OpenRift is. Seeds do not follow. When the derived qualification differs from the stored seeds, the standings tab shows "Seeds were locked when the cut was generated" with the differing rows marked. Regenerating the cut means deleting the cut rounds from the last one back, the existing re-roll action, after which the next generation reseeds.

### Surfaces

- Pairings view during the group stage: one section per group (the paired 3-player groups share one), its matches by round and the group's progress. Self-paced: a "Start round N" action per group that enables once its previous round is fully reported. Default: one "Start round N" for all groups in the page header, enabled once every group has reported. A "cross-group" tag marks the paired groups' shared match. Cut rounds use a bracket layout.
- Report-token page: the same per-group layout, with result entry, and the per-group start action when self-paced.
- Standings tab: one table per group, per-group progress, a qualification list with seeds after the group stage, the bracket after round 4. "Generate top 8" stays disabled naming the groups still playing.
- Participants tab: a Legend column, editable until groups are generated; group and slot are read-only columns once generated.
- Group generation: a check dialog listing participants without a Legend, with inline entry and a "skip the Legend tiebreak" action.
- Standings: an "Enter meta shares" dialog, reachable only while a tie waits at the meta tier, listing the affected Legends with one input each.
- Follow-token pages inherit all of the above through the same presenters.

## More Information

Out of scope, deliberately:

- Fetching meta share from any deck site. The meta tier exists, but its values are typed in by the organizer per tournament.
- Group sizes other than four, and cut sizes other than 4, 8 and 16.
- Byes in the group stage. The player count must fill the groups.
- 2v2 play and pod pairing in this format.

Anyone holding the report token may start a group's next round, the same trust the token grants for result entry; the players at the table are the intended users.
